**INT. KALIO DUPLEX, PETER ODILI ROAD, PORT HARCOURT. NIGHT**

Rain hammers the windows. A promotion party is in full swing: twenty guests, a generator humming behind the wall, glasses clinking. RICHARD KALIO (38), tight burgundy shirt, gold-rimmed sunglasses pushed up into his fade, holds a glass high and turns a heavy gold watch on his wrist. At his elbow stands STEPHANIE SEKIBO (29), fuchsia dress, glossy bob, long French-tip nails.

ESTHER KALIO (33), in a plain sage-green wrapper and blouse, crosses the room with a tray of fried plantain and small chops, her head down.

RICHARD
(laughing, loud, glass raised)


Look at her! A primary school teacher, carrying plantain in my sitting room like a house girl. Esther, who asked you to serve my guests?

ESTHER
(quiet, balancing the tray)
You did, Richard. This morning.

RICHARD
(cold, the smile gone)
I asked you to stay in the kitchen. Not to stand where my colleagues can see you.

STEPHANIE
(sweet, poisonous, touching his sleeve)
Richard, leave her. She is trying. Not everybody can be at this level.

Esther's eyes go to Stephanie's hand on his sleeve, then back to Richard. She sets the tray down on the side table, beside a brass-capped fountain pen lying on a folded exercise book.

RICHARD
(stepping closer, voice rising so every guest hears)
Five years I have carried this woman. Her whole salary cannot fuel my generator for one month. Today they made me Deputy General Manager. Do you understand? I cannot stand on that stage with a woman who smells of chalk.

ESTHER
(very calm)
What are you saying, Richard?

RICHARD
(pointing at the door)
Pack one bag. Go to your people in Warri. Go and sit in that falling house in Ekpan.

ESTHER
In this rain? At night?

RICHARD
(spreading his arms, smiling at the guests)
This roof is mine. Every plate, every light, every kobo in this house is my salary.

He sweeps his hand across the side table. The brass pen flies off and rolls across the tiles.

RICHARD (CONT'D)
(sneering)
And take that market trinket with you. Seven years you carry it like a staff of office.

The pen stops against Stephanie's heel. She smiles and does not move. Esther crosses the floor, bends, picks it up, and wipes it slowly on her wrapper. The room goes quiet. Only the generator hums.

ESTHER
(quietly, almost to herself)
My father's pen is not a trinket.

RICHARD
(shrugging, laughing to the room)
Ladies and gentlemen, my wife, the poet!

ESTHER
(lifting her bag from the chair, steady)
You are sure, Richard? Before all these people?

RICHARD
(arms wide)
I have never been surer. Without me, you will not eat.

Esther slides the pen into her pocket and opens the door. Rain blows in across the tiles.

ESTHER
(not turning around)
Tonight, when everyone has gone home, open your payslip. Read the bottom line. See whose name carries it.

The door closes. Richard laughs, but it is only half a laugh. His thumb stops turning the gold watch.

CUT TO BLACK.
