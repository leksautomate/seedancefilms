CLIP A (0:04-0:55)
HOOK [0:04-0:22]
Characters on screen: Qutuz (adult), Elder Emir

INT. QUTUZ'S TENT, SALIHIYYA CAMP, NIGHT, 1259. One torch. Qutuz stands over a map of Syria. An ELDER EMIR waits at the tent flap.

ELDER EMIR: They will say a slave took the throne of Egypt.

QUTUZ: Let them. The Mongols never ask who sits on a throne. They ask if it stands.

ELDER EMIR: And the boy sultan?

QUTUZ: A boy cannot lift a sword.

Qutuz's eyes catch the torch. SMASH CUT. TEXT: Damascus. Many years earlier.

SCENE 1: THE MARKET [0:22-0:55]
Characters on screen: Qutuz (child), Slave Dealer, Buyer

EXT. DAMASCUS SLAVE MARKET, MIDDAY. Dust, shouting, rows of chained children. A DEALER shoves a thin boy onto a wooden block.

DEALER: Step up, boy. Tell the buyers your name.

YOUNG QUTUZ: Mahmud, son of Mamdud. My uncle was Khwarazm-Shah, a king.

DEALER: Everyone in this market was a king yesterday.

A BUYER in a fine coat circles the boy.

BUYER: Does he fight, or does he cry?

YOUNG QUTUZ: I do not cry.

BUYER: Then he is worth the coin.

Close-up: the boy's eyes as the chain closes around his wrist.

YOUNG QUTUZ: (to himself) Then I will build a home no one can sell.

CLIP B (0:55-1:35)
SCENE 2: THE DEAD SULTAN
Characters on screen: Qutuz (adult), Shajar al-Durr, Sultan Aybak (body)

TEXT: Cairo Citadel, 1257. INT. ROYAL BATH CHAMBER, NIGHT. Steam, lamplight. SULTAN AYBAK lies still on the marble. SHAJAR AL-DURR stands over him, veiled and calm. Qutuz enters, breathing hard.

QUTUZ: The sultan is dead. Who did this?

SHAJAR AL-DURR: Egypt is not a house for the soft, Qutuz. Someone had to decide.

QUTUZ: You decided with a blade, and now every emir will want one.

SHAJAR AL-DURR: Then be the one who holds the sword, not the one it falls on.

QUTUZ: (quietly) Aybak made me an emir. I will not be his grave-digger.

SHAJAR AL-DURR: Then protect his son. The boy is fifteen, and the wolves are already circling.

Qutuz looks down at the dead sultan's face.

QUTUZ: I swear it.

CLIP C (1:35-2:30)
SCENE 3: THE BOY ON THE THRONE
Characters on screen: Qutuz (adult), Sultan Ali, Elder Emir, Rider, Guards

INT. CITADEL THRONE ROOM, 1259. Autumn light. SULTAN ALI sits on a throne too big for him. Qutuz stands at his shoulder. A RIDER, covered in dust, kneels.

RIDER: Sultan, the Mongols are moving west. Hulagu marches on Syria.

ELDER EMIR: Egypt cannot fight an army that took Baghdad.

ALI: (small voice) Atabek, what do we do?

QUTUZ: We do not hide behind a crown, my lord.

ALI: Then I will lead them.

QUTUZ: With respect, you cannot hold a bow for a day, and a Mongol arrow does not wait.

ALI: Am I being put away?

QUTUZ: You are being kept safe. Egypt needs a sword, and no one has ever given you one.

ALI: (quiet) Aybak was my father.

QUTUZ: And I swore to him that you would live. You will live.

Guards escort Ali out. His robe drags across the stone.

ELDER EMIR: History will call this a theft.

QUTUZ: History will call it Egypt, if Egypt lives.

CLIP D (2:30-3:00)
SCENE 4: THE WALL AT NIGHT
Characters on screen: Qutuz (adult), Elder Emir, Rider

EXT. CITADEL WALL, NIGHT, JANUARY 1260. Wind. Qutuz looks north across the dark plain. The Elder Emir climbs the steps behind him.

ELDER EMIR: The emirs have sworn their loyalty, my lord. Half from fear, half from hope.

QUTUZ: Fear and hope will do. I need one thing more.

ELDER EMIR: What is that?

QUTUZ: A reason to be remembered for something other than a crown.

Hooves. A RIDER races up the stairs, breathless.

RIDER: My lord, the Mongols have reached Aleppo! The siege has begun!

QUTUZ: Then the clock has started.

SMASH CUT TO BLACK.

PRODUCTION NOTES
Debated history: Qutuz's royal-origin claim (Mahmud, nephew of Khwarazm-Shah) comes from later tradition. The scene frames it as the boy's own claim, which the dealer ignores.
Aybak's death (1257): sources attribute it to Shajar al-Durr's orders. Shown as an aftermath scene only.
Ali's removal: Qutuz took the throne in late 1259 citing the Mongol threat. Ali's later fate is kept off screen.
Signature clip shot: the boy's chain closing, cut to the adult's eyes.
